# RFC 0216 — Risk register

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Score | Mitigation | Owner | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | The exception grows into a general escape from `blocked`: rows that are merely inconvenient to witness in production are added to the list. | M | H | High | The list is a corpus file under a gate (§A.3). It takes exact ids only, has a closed anchor enum, needs an Active/Accepted RFC with its own rows table for each entry, requires the scenario to actually read the anchor variable, and excludes every floor scenario. Only the anchor-reason `blocked` is eligible (§A.4), and the RFC 0147 §A.6 full window applies to any RFC that extends it. | Conformance Architect | `mitigated` — by §A.3 and §A.4, once implemented at `Active`. |
| R2 | A dishonest or careless operator cuts the companion from the same image with different configuration: a flag that disables the verifier's lifetime comparison, not visible in discovery. The companion passes, and production does not do what the row says. | L | H | Medium | §C.10(g) refuses every difference discovery exposes, and §C.10(e) makes the operator's published key sign the companion. What remains is operator self-evidence (`evidenceTier: self`), the same trust every self-cut bundle already carries. | Security Architect | `accepted` — residual operator trust, named in §D.13 and Unresolved question 4 (gap G3). |
| R3 | A companion that trusts a runner-held issuer is exposed to the internet, or is given production data or credentials, and becomes an authentication bypass for whatever it can reach. | L | H | Medium | §F's GOVERNANCE prose forbids both, and the openwop-app recipe binds loopback only, with memory storage and throwaway secrets. Not observable from the bundle: the falsifiability row is `unwitnessable` and says why. | Security Architect | `mitigated` — by the operator obligation; it cannot be witnessed. |
| R4 | Verifiers on suites that predate `Active` reject production bundles carrying `witnessed-colocated`, so a third party pinned to an older suite cannot read them. | M | L | Low | This fails closed: an older verifier cannot misread the row as a pass. Consumers pin `@openwop/spec-artifacts`, and a bundle is evidence at its own suite version (RFC 0197 §C). | Compatibility Architect | `accepted` — the safe direction of the strict-consumer hazard (RFC 0171 G6). |
| R5 | Option A puts the production bundle-signing private key on the colocated rig. | M | L | Low | The key signs certification bundles only and authenticates nothing on the host. The rig is the operator's own machine, where production bundles are already cut. Option B (gap G2) removes the need. | Security Architect | `accepted` — pending the G2 decision. |
| R6 | Production redeploys after the cut, and the certified production bundle then describes an image that no longer serves. | M | M | Medium | The same holds for every production bundle today. `implementation.build` makes the drift detectable by re-fetching discovery, and GOVERNANCE already requires a flip to be checked against the live surface. §C.10(f) bounds the companion to 7 days of the production cut. | Conformance Architect | `mitigated` — by §C.11 and the existing live check at the flip. |
