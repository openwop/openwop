# RFC 0138 — Risk register

Scored Likelihood × Impact (H/M/L). Critical/High risks carry a named mitigation
owner and a resolution path.

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Score | Mitigation | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **"Opaque" is read as "stash it for later."** A host retains an unrecognized extension and later renders it, interpolates it into a prompt, or interprets it as markup. Because the value is pack-authored and untrusted, this converts a *loud publication failure* (rejected at registry `PUT`) into a *silent injection surface* (at render) — strictly worse than having no hatch at all. This is the risk the whole RFC turns on. | M | H | **High** | The "ignore means ignore" clause is normative, not advisory, and enumerates the forbidden actions (render, execute, interpret-as-markup, code-path-switch, persist-into-an-interpreted-surface) rather than gesturing at them. Backed by the `pack-manifest-extension-opaque` invariant and a threat-model row. **Residual: enforcement is corpus-side only — see G7.** | Security Architect | Open (mitigated, not closed) |
| R2 | **The hatch is later widened.** A well-meaning change to `^.*` or `^x-` alone would silently defeat typo detection, which is `additionalProperties: false`'s real job — `dispalyName` becomes a silent no-op instead of a rejection. | L | H | Med | Conformance sweeps *every* closed object in *every* manifest and asserts no hatch pattern matches a bare canonical-looking key. Sabotage-verified: widening to `^.*` reds 3 legs. | Conformance Architect | **Closed** — regression-guarded |
| R3 | **A new pack kind ships without the hatch**, silently reintroducing the contradiction for that kind only. This nearly happened during authoring: the first draft of the scenario enumerated manifests by naming convention (`*-pack-manifest.schema.json`) and skipped `frontend-plugin-manifest.schema.json` because its name lacks `-pack-`. | M | M | Med | The scenario asserts an explicit in-scope count (9), not a glob. Adding a manifest fails the leg until the author either wires the hatch or names the exclusion in `OUT_OF_SCOPE` with a reason. | Conformance Architect | **Closed** — the near-miss is the test case |
| R4 | **Extensions become a de-facto capability channel.** A host infers support from an extension's presence, or makes handling of a canonical field depend on one. Packs then stop being portable in practice while remaining portable on paper — the exact failure the RFC exists to prevent, arriving through the door it opened. | M | H | **High** | Explicit MUST NOT in the normative prose, restated in the invariant note and the threat-model row. Reinforced structurally by refusing to define a capability flag for extension support (§Compatibility), so there is nothing to negotiate against. **Residual: behavioral, undetectable server-free — see G7.** | Spec Architect | Open (mitigated, not closed) |
| R5 | **The registry split-brain recurs at another layer.** A validation surface not covered here (a future mirror schema, an indexer's own copy of the shape) closes its root and re-creates "validates locally, rejected at publish." | L | M | Low | The registry publication contract is in scope and asserted by the same count-based leg as the source manifests. Measured, not assumed: a root-level probe returned `must NOT have additional properties` before the fix. Any new mirror schema matching `*manifest*.schema.json` is auto-enrolled by the scenario. | Schema Architect | **Closed** for known surfaces |
| R6 | **Namespace collision between two vendors** claiming the same `x-` key, producing cross-host misinterpretation of the same bytes. | L | M | Low | `host-extensions.md` recommends `private.<host>.*` for host-internal use. Escalation path is a registered-extensions registry (§Alternatives 4), deliberately deferred — building it pre-emptively reimposes the governance friction `x-` prefixes exist to avoid. See G1. | Spec Architect | Accepted risk |
| R7 | **Manifest bloat.** No per-property size cap; a pack ships a multi-megabyte extension blob. | L | L | Low | Transitively bounded by the existing tarball + manifest size caps in `SECURITY/threat-model-node-packs.md`. A second cap would be a second place to drift. See G2. | Security Architect | Accepted risk |
| R8 | **Extensions are silently dropped in transit.** A host that installs and re-exports a pack loses its extensions, since retention is permitted but not required. | L | M | Low | Acknowledged as G3 rather than specified. No host round-trips packs today, so requiring retention would be writing a contract against zero implementations. | Compatibility Architect | Deferred |
